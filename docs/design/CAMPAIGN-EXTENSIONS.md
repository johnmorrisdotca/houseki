# Magnetic Blocks, Shizen and Arashi campaigns

Each extension has a deliberate complete count of 50 challenges. Fifty leaves room for distinct introductory, intermediate and advanced techniques without padding the set with renamed seeds, recolours or reflections. Tutorials are separate from this count. The four original campaigns retain their 300 challenges.

## Campaign rules

| Campaign | Player decisions | Required mechanical evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Magnetic Blocks | Rotate and place bonded squares; choose when to spend the Floor Switch; plan around authored Calm/Pull schedules and selected impact drops | Real bonded or split settling, with floor-dependent outcomes in the teaching and advanced sets |
| Shizen | Place pairs around marked stones; anticipate attraction; choose careful placement versus power-drop rebound | A witnessed attraction or rebound transition, with meaningful magnetic placement choices |
| Arashi | Plan finite pair placements around scheduled earthquakes and lightning | A weather event before victory, with consequences for the board and the remaining plan |

Challenges use exact original starting boards, queues, seeds, goals and declared modifiers. They do not fall on a real-time clock. Each supplied witness must reach the first victory through accepted actions under the shipped rules. Finite queues end in a clear loss when the target is not met. Goals show the target, current count and a progress bar. The result panel names the outcome, disables play controls and offers replay or the next level.

## Generation and grading

Generate a larger deterministic candidate pool, reject unsolved or duplicate boards, measure legal and successful choices through the actual engine, then curate and order the levels. Canonical keys account for colour relabeling and horizontal reflection; technique-specific flags and rules remain part of identity. Store raw measurements, score, grading revision, witness and checksum beside stable level IDs.

The difficulty score must include measured choice or forgiveness. Planning length counts committed pieces and consequential gadget decisions, never the number of left/right keypresses. Multi-placement plans and mechanic counterfactuals distinguish preparation from an immediate payoff. Assign the five marks from the stored score using the family formula, and number only after ordering. Opening examples should introduce one concept; the final examples should require the strongest planning in the curated set. Grades remain subject to human calibration.

## Player and release checks

Campaign selection displays its own level list and objective in English and Japanese. Fixed dimensions, colours and modifiers visibly match the running puzzle and cannot be silently overridden by free-play settings. Returning to free play restores those preferences. Changing a pending campaign does not relabel the active run. Saves restore the exact campaign and stable level identity; restart restores its original queue and board. Completion advances within the same campaign.

Verify every witness independently, deterministic regeneration, canonical uniqueness, grades/order, first and last difficulty bands, rejection of malformed options, and terminal behavior. Browser checks cover all three selectors, representative witnessed wins, next level, loss, restart, exact recovery, both languages and phone/desktop geometry. Existing basic campaigns, Daily mode, themes, piece styles, keyboard/keypad input and reduced motion retain their behavior.

Publish the typed APIs, regenerated API reference, updated README and MIT package together. Deployment must be checked on the actual `/houseki/` public path. Physical-device feel, fluent translation review and subjective difficulty calibration remain separate human reviews. Itsutsu integration is outside this package work.
